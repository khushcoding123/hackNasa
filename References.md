# References

**MOONMESH related work and source register — researched 14 November 2026**

MOONMESH compares lunar south-pole landing points, calculates terrain-aware Sun and direct-to-Earth (DTE) visibility over time, screens surface relay sites for two-hop communication and power, and evaluates outage and single-relay-failure outcomes. This is a targeted survey of the most relevant public projects, papers, technical reports, and source products found through NASA, ESA, JPL/NAIF, arXiv, and publisher records. It is not a claim that every publication in the field has been found. Each link goes to the publisher, author, agency, or data provider where possible. Descriptions below summarize what the sources say; the comparisons to MOONMESH are our assessment.

## Closest prior work

| Work | What it already does | Relationship to MOONMESH |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Bryant (2009), *Lunar Pole Illumination and Communications Maps Computed from GSSR Elevation Data*](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20150014706) | Uses a polar DEM and local horizon masks to estimate both solar illumination and Earth visibility at possible south-pole base sites. | Direct precedent for terrain-based Sun/Earth visibility; uses radar DEM and long-run maps rather than MOONMESH's selected-date surface-relay planning. |
| [NASA Goddard, *Lunar Polar Illumination*](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/69) | Publishes LOLA-derived illumination and Earth-visibility maps, discusses finite-disc visibility, height, timescale, and DEM resolution. | Strong reference for validating the geometry and for explaining why a time-specific result can differ from a long-term average. Its Earth-visible definition is deliberately more optimistic than a specific ground-station radio link. |
| [NASA Glenn, *Lunar Surface Network Relay Terminal: A Lunar Communications Platform* (2023)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230013361) and [siting presentation](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230014675) | Designs stationary and mobile surface terminals with solar/battery or radioisotope power. The presentation evaluates a grid of candidate tower locations at Malapert Massif for terrain line of sight and coverage. | Very close precedent for a powered surface relay and candidate siting. MOONMESH adds its own date-specific DTE outage, local two-hop, screened-count, and failure workflow; it should not claim the surface-relay concept or terrain-based siting as new. |
| [NASA Glenn, *Lunar Surface Relay – Mobile* (2023)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230012703) | Studies a relocatable surface relay and solar/battery versus radioisotope power options to serve changing Artemis sortie sites. | Prior engineering treatment of relay mobility and darkness survival. MOONMESH uses simplified static candidate and battery assumptions. |
| [NASA Glenn, *BEACON Demonstrator* (2025)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240014584) | Conceptual south-pole tower combining elevated solar arrays, 3GPP surface relay, backhaul, and laser power beaming; service range depends on terrain. | Close hardware precedent for a powered mast relay. Its backhaul can use lunar orbital relays, which is architecturally different from MOONMESH's independent DTE relay path. |
| [Santorelli and Wyglinski (2026), *LunarRF*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34554) | GPU terrain-aware surface RF propagation engine and pole-wide 20 m coverage atlas across several bands, candidate masts, LOS, best server, and redundancy. | Strongest recent precedent for pole-wide surface RF coverage and candidate masts. MOONMESH addresses a narrower, time-dependent landing-site DTE and power/resilience question; LunarRF has a more detailed propagation model. Preprint, not evidence of peer review. |
| [Montano and Bussey (2022), *Ray Tracing Techniques for the Characterization of Lunar Communication Architectures*](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210019407) | Uses GPU ray tracing and terrain to estimate lunar communication contact time, outage time, and received data rate across architectures. | Direct precedent for terrain-aware communications windows and outage metrics. MOONMESH's simpler horizon and chord-LOS model should be described as a planning approximation. |
| [NASA, *Endurance Mission Concept Study*, Appendix K](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/endurance-spa-traverse-and-sample-return.pdf) | Describes a tool combining SPICE, high-resolution DEMs, paths, and relay-orbiter geometry to find rover communication windows. | Close method precedent for ephemeris plus terrain plus communication timing, though focused on a rover and orbital relay. |

## Polar illumination, terrain horizons, and landing-site studies

1. [Mazarico et al. (2011), *Illumination conditions of the lunar polar regions using LOLA topography*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2010.10.030). Foundational LOLA horizon-based polar illumination modeling over the lunar precession cycle. Use to benchmark the horizon method, long-period averaging, and treatment of low-angle sunlight; it is not a relay optimizer. The [NASA product page](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/69) explains the related released maps.
2. [Bussey et al. (2010), *Illumination conditions of the south pole of the Moon derived using Kaguya topography*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2010.03.028). Earlier terrain-based polar lighting study using Kaguya altimetry and checks against images. Useful independent terrain/illumination precedent.
3. [De Rosa et al. (2012), *Characterisation of Potential Landing Sites for the European Space Agency's Lunar Lander Project*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.5587). Uses LOLA and three independent illumination tools, then evaluates slopes, craters, and boulders for candidate south-pole landing sites. Particularly relevant to MOONMESH's candidate-site comparison and the distinction between regional screening and a qualified landing point.
4. [Gläser et al. (2014), *Illumination conditions at the lunar south pole using high resolution Digital Terrain Models from LOLA*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2014.08.013). Studies 20 m terrain, imaging cross-checks, observation height, and long-term lighting at specific sites. The height/resolution sensitivity is relevant to MOONMESH's mast and DEM assumptions.
5. [Speyerer and Robinson (2013), *Persistently illuminated regions at the lunar poles: Ideal sites for future exploration*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2012.10.010). LROC-image-based illumination mapping, an observational comparison for terrain simulations. Listed in [NASA's LRO publication record](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/lro/lro-publications/).
6. [Barker et al. (2021), *Improved LOLA elevation maps for south pole landing sites: Error estimates and their impact on illumination conditions*](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20205009660). Quantifies DEM height/slope uncertainty and its effect on horizon and illumination estimates. Direct support for MOONMESH's uncertainty and DEM-bias reporting; a pixel spacing is not the same as independent ground truth at that scale.
7. [Tong et al. (2023), *A high-precision horizon-based illumination modeling method for the lunar surface using pyramidal LOLA data*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2022.115302). Multiresolution horizon calculation and finite solar-disc treatment. Relevant method comparison for MOONMESH's near/far DEM sampling and binary Sun visibility.
8. [NASA, *PGDA South Pole LOLA DEM Mosaic*](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/81). Higher-resolution south-pole DEM products and associated uncertainty work. A possible future dataset for site-level checks, subject to its coverage and product metadata.

## Mission planners, maps, and communication services

9. [NASA/JPL, *Moon Trek*](https://trek.nasa.gov/moon/). Public lunar map with distance, elevation-profile, Sun-angle, slope, surface-lighting, and line-of-sight tools; some advanced mission-planning tools require an account. Comparable interactive exploration and a useful independent visual cross-check, but not a documented equivalent of MOONMESH's constrained relay and N−1 workflow.
10. [LROC/ASU, *QuickMap*](https://quickmap.lroc.asu.edu/). Lunar image and geospatial-layer viewer used for landing-site inspection. Useful for checking terrain, slopes, illumination layers, and whether a purported site is a point or a broad region; see the [LROC QuickMap project description](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/images/977).
11. [NASA, *VIPER Lunar Operations*](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/viper/lunar-operations/). Describes operational planning around light, terrain, DTE blackout periods, and safe havens for a south-pole rover. Operational precedent for reporting blackout duration and power survival together, rather than only mean visibility.
12. [NASA, *Lunar Communications and Navigation Architecture: Human Lunar Return* (2024)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230016359). Architecture context for DTE limitations and orbital relay services. Crucial boundary: a surface relay with its own Earth link cannot restore DTE when Earth is geometrically unavailable to all feasible nearby surface sites; an orbital backhaul or other architecture is needed for that period.
13. [NASA, *LunaNet and LCRNS* (2025 presentation)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250002120). Shows modeled south-pole DTE versus orbital-relay coverage over a ten-year LOS simulation, with specified site assumptions. Use as architecture context, not as numerical validation of MOONMESH's surface-relay outputs.
14. [ESA, *Moonlight*](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Connectivity_and_Secure_Communications/Moonlight2) and [*Lunar Pathfinder*](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/human-and-robotic-exploration/moon/lunar-science-tech-demos/lunar-pathfinder). Planned lunar orbital communications/navigation services prioritizing the south pole. These address the broader backhaul gap that local surface DTE relays cannot cover; their service claims should not be conflated with MOONMESH's surface-only topology.
15. [NASA, *Lunar Surface Propagation*](https://www.nasa.gov/glenn/glenn-expertise-space-exploration/scan/lunar-surface-propagation/). Agency overview of low Earth elevation, terrain, and propagation challenges. Context for why simple free-space RF margin is an initial screen, not a field-validated link budget.

## Data, ephemerides, and site provenance

16. [Smith et al. (2010), *The Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter Investigation on the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Mission*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11214-009-9512-y). Instrument/data provenance for LOLA. MOONMESH uses the [LOLA polar gridded products](https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lro/lro-l-lola-2-edr-v1/lrolol_0xxx/document/archsis.htm), including a 20 m south-polar DEM and a coarser far-field DEM; cite the exact product labels in any published result.
17. [JPL/NAIF, *Generic Kernels*](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/naif/data_generic.html) and [*Lunar Frames Kernel*](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/toolkit_docs/Tutorials/pdf/individual_docs/23_lunar-earth_pck-fk.pdf). Source for the DE440 SPICE geometry and the lunar mean-Earth frame mapping used by MOONMESH. The [SPICE Geometry Finder documentation](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/toolkit_docs/C/req/gf.html) is a useful reference for event transitions and apparent/geometric distinctions.
18. [NASA (2024), *Artemis III Moon Landing Regions*](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-provides-update-on-artemis-iii-moon-landing-regions/). Official regions, not official coordinates for MOONMESH's individual sample points. Label Haworth, Nobile Rim 1, and Malapert coordinates in the app as approximate research points unless a separately documented exact site is supplied.

## What this survey implies for MOONMESH

- **Established methods and concepts:** DEM-based Sun/Earth horizons, illumination/site comparison, terrain-sensitive DTE windows, surface relay towers, power storage, and relay candidate siting all have public precedent in the sources above. Do not present any of those elements alone as novel.
- **Defensible project description:** MOONMESH is an integrated, reproducible *planning prototype* that places selected-date sunlight, terrain-aware DTE outage attribution, simple two-hop surface-relay feasibility, screened candidate optimization, and one-relay failure analysis in one local workflow. This is a description of its current feature combination, not a claim that no other tool does it.
- **Important architecture limit:** MOONMESH's surface relays need their own Earth link. They can route around local terrain blockage if a candidate sees Earth, but they cannot guarantee communication during region-wide Earth occultation. NASA and ESA orbital relay studies solve a different, larger problem.
- **Important model limit:** For a quantitative comparison with LunarRF or NASA architecture studies, match the same site, DEM, epoch, antenna heights, link budget, propagation effects, ground station or Earth-disc definition, and outage threshold. Their reported percentages are not interchangeable with MOONMESH's results.
- **Most useful follow-up validation:** Compare one MOONMESH horizon against the LOLA/PGDA products, one blackout transition against a finer time step or SPICE geometry check, and one candidate surface LOS/RF result against a higher-fidelity propagation model. Record the exact coordinate and DEM product for every comparison.
